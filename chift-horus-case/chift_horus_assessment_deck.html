Why: stored number, status and update date come only from Horus

Horus connector assessment · Chift product/tech case, Part 15 / 21

This is the pattern behind most routes, shown on invoice creation. Chift sends one request. Before posting, the connector needs Horus's own references: the daybook, the accounts, the analytic codes, and the folder's legislation, which selects the VAT list. Those are cached per folder, but fiscal years are read fresh, because the accountant can close a period at any time. Then it checks the partner and, as a best-effort proposal, whether the number already exists. The create returns only an id, so we read the entry back. Likely question: 'Why so many calls?' Most are cached reference data, and a wrong invoice can't be deleted through the API, so checking first is cheaper.

How Horus differs

Similar words mean different things in Chift and Horus

Horus has its own vocabulary and rules; most effort and risk come from these differences, not from missing objects.

Client / supplierOne 'company' record, flagged customer and/or supplierSearch key must be generated; force_merge flags an existing supplier as customer
JournalDaybook; its type decides which endpoint can post into itDaybook type is the routing key for every write route

VAT codeNo VAT-code entity: a combination of rate, origin and typeNeeds a translation table (Proposal: connector-built list, C2)

In Horus, a 'company' is a customer or supplier inside a folder, never the folder's own company.
Horus connector assessment · Chift product/tech case, Part 16 / 21

Most mapping mistakes come from words that look alike. In Horus, a 'company' is a third party the folder deals with, a customer or supplier, not the folder's own company. One record can be both, which is why Chift's force_merge fits well. A journal is a daybook, and its type decides which endpoint can post into it. Analytic plans are cost schemas, and Horus stores amounts on combinations, not percentages. There is no VAT-code entity at all. And pairing 'posted' with 'confirmed' is my proposal, not something the documentation states. Likely question: 'Why not map posted to confirmed directly?' Horus keeps API entries unconfirmed for the accountant's review; that's Chift's call, C1.

This is the most important transformation. Chift sends one tax_code per invoice line, the id of a VAT code. Horus has no such code: each line carries a rate, an origin and a type, plus kind and condition on purchases, and only the combinations in two published spreadsheets are accepted; the counts are on the slide. Outstanding items show the asymmetry best. For clients, Horus has a ready list of open sales invoices with a documented open amount; we join it with customer ledger lines to get the account and the matching number. For suppliers there is no equivalent, so the connector computes open amounts from ledger lines not yet matched. The documentation does not say whether partially matched lines are returned. If they are not, partly paid supplier invoices silently disappear, which is why H8 is a before-build question. The first test: one partly paid purchase invoice and one partly paid sales invoice. Likely question: one call per supplier? No, provided that leaving out the company filter returns every partner, also H8.

Recommendation
